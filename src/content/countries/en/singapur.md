## Strongest in the Primary Grades

For primary teachers, Singapore is especially compelling because it leads the world specifically on fourth-grade assessments. In TIMSS 2023, Singaporean fourth-graders scored 615 in mathematics and 607 in science — 168 and 159 points ahead of fourth-graders in Bosnia and Herzegovina. In PIRLS 2021, Singapore ranked first in reading and was the only country to improve across all five assessment cycles.

## Everything Pulls Together

Singapore's curriculum is centralized, and curriculum documents, approved textbooks, classroom instruction, and national exams reinforce one another closely. What teachers teach in class matches what sits in the textbook and what is assessed on exams. Primary mathematics follows the Concrete–Pictorial–Abstract (CPA) progression and teaches children to represent word problems visually using bar models before jumping to equations.

## Teachers

Every public-school teacher prepares at a single institution, the National Institute of Education (NIE). Candidates are recruited from roughly the top third of their graduating cohort, supported by structured career pathways — teaching, leadership, or curriculum specialist tracks — and dedicated annual hours for professional development.

## Easing Exam Pressure

At the end of Primary 6, students sit the national Primary School Leaving Examination (PSLE). Since 2021, fine-grained T-scores have been replaced by broader Achievement Levels (AL1–AL8 per subject) so families are not competing over single-point differences. In 2024, Singapore also abolished rigid secondary streams: under Full Subject-Based Banding, students take each subject at the level suited to them (G1–G3) and can move between levels over time. The centralized Gifted Education Programme (GEP) is likewise being replaced with school-based high-ability programs across all primary schools.

## The Cost of High Stakes

Exam pressure remains real, and private after-school tutoring is widespread. Much of Singapore's recent reform agenda aims specifically to soften early sorting without giving up high academic standards.

## What We Could Borrow

- **Tight alignment between curriculum, textbooks, and assessments**, so learning outcomes actually reach classrooms instead of staying on paper.
- **The Concrete–Pictorial–Abstract progression and bar modeling** in early-grades mathematics.
- **Systematic screening and small-group support** for struggling readers and math learners right in Grade 1.
