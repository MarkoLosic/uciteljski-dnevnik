## Starting School at Age Four

Dutch children enter primary school (basisschool) at age four, with compulsory attendance starting at five. Primary school runs through eight “groups”: the first two function as play-based early years, while groep 3 corresponds to first grade elsewhere. After groep 8, around age 12, students are placed into one of three secondary tracks: vocational (VMBO), senior general (HAVO), or pre-university (VWO).

Placement rests on the primary teacher's recommendation alongside the doorstroomtoets, a national transition test taken in February of the final year. Introduced in 2024, it replaced the earlier end-of-primary eindtoets.

## Freedom of Education

Article 23 of the Dutch Constitution guarantees freedom of education. As a result, most schools are run by private boards — religious, Montessori, Dalton, Jenaplan, or non-denominational — yet receive full public funding on equal footing with municipal schools. The government sets core attainment targets (kerndoelen) and the Inspectorate monitors school quality, leaving each school free to choose its own teaching methods and materials.

## Teacher Shortages

As of October 1, 2025, primary schools were short the equivalent of 5,760 full-time teachers — a vacancy rate of 6.3%. In the five largest cities, the shortage reached 13.7%, more than double the rate elsewhere in the country. Primary enrollment is projected to edge down slightly through 2031.

## Phones Out of the Classroom

Starting in the 2024/25 school year, a national agreement between the ministry and school organizations removed phones, tablets, and smartwatches from primary classrooms except when explicitly needed for learning. Rather than a statutory ban backed by fines, it works as a shared sector-wide rule.

## Student Results

Mathematics remains a clear strength: in PISA 2025, the Netherlands scored 483 — well above the OECD average — with 13% of students reaching the top proficiency levels. In TIMSS 2023, Dutch fourth-graders posted the narrowest gap between highest and lowest performers of any participating system. Reading, however, has fallen sharply to 441 points — down 18 points since 2022 and 62 points since 2015, roughly equivalent to nearly three years of schooling. Fully 39% of 15-year-olds now score below baseline reading proficiency.

## What We Could Borrow

- **Clear state-level learning goals paired with school freedom over teaching methods.**
- **A straightforward sector-wide agreement keeping phones out of primary classrooms** as a fast, low-cost step.
- **A warning from the reading slump:** pedagogical autonomy cannot mean spending less time on sustained reading practice.
