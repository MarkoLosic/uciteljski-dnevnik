## Sixteen State Systems

In Germany, school education falls under the authority of the 16 federal states (Länder). Each state writes its own curricula and runs its own school supervision, while the Standing Conference of Education Ministers (KMK) agrees on common national standards — including benchmarks for the end of grade 4. That makes Germany an instructive comparison for Bosnia and Herzegovina: decentralized governance can still operate around shared national standards.

## Tracking After Grade 4

Primary school (Grundschule) lasts just four years in most of Germany, and six only in Berlin and Brandenburg. Around age ten, children are sorted into different secondary schools: academic Gymnasium, intermediate Realschule, vocational-oriented Hauptschule, or an integrated school combining tracks. It is one of the earliest tracking points in the OECD, and family background weighs heavily on student outcomes. In PISA 2025, only 2% of socioeconomically disadvantaged students reached top performance levels, compared with roughly 25% of advantaged peers.

## Diagnostic Tests Without Stakes

Across Germany, third- and eighth-graders sit VERA comparative assessments designed to show teachers where their classes stand against national standards. They are strictly diagnostic tools rather than high-stakes exams.

## Lowest PISA Scores on Record

In PISA 2025, Germany scored 464 in mathematics and 465 in reading — its lowest results since PISA began, falling below even its 2000 "PISA shock" baseline. Roughly 34% of 15-year-olds missed baseline proficiency in mathematics and around 32% in reading. Fourth-grade reading also dropped by 13 points in PIRLS 2021.

## What Is Changing

- **Legal right to full-day primary care (Ganztag):** Starting August 1, 2026, every child entering grade 1 is legally entitled to eight hours per day, five days a week — including lessons — plus school-holiday coverage with at most four weeks closed per year. The entitlement expands one grade per year to cover grades 1–4 by 2029/30. The federal government is contributing up to 3.5 billion euros for facilities and sharing operating costs, though staffing and space remain tight in many municipalities.
- **Startchancen-Programm:** Launched in 2024/25, this ten-year, 20-billion-euro federal-state program channels targeted funding to just over 4,000 schools serving high shares of disadvantaged students — roughly 10% of Germany's student body.

## What We Could Borrow

- **Binding end-of-grade-4 standards** across a decentralized system, while leaving local authorities wide room on organization.
- **Diagnostic testing in grade 3** so primary teachers spot learning gaps while there is still time to close them.
- **Concentrating extra funding on high-need schools** instead of spreading small amounts evenly everywhere.

**What not to borrow:** sorting ten-year-olds into separate school tracks. Germany's own data show how strongly early tracking locks in family inequality.
