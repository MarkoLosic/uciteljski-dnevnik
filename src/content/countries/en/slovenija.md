## Why Slovenia Matters to Our Region

Slovenia is closest to Bosnia and Herzegovina in both language and shared school tradition, and for decades it posted the strongest international results in the former Yugoslav space. Today it offers a sobering lesson in how quickly a solid system can lose ground.

## How School Is Organized

Slovenia's nine-year single-structure basic school (devetletka), introduced in the 2000s, is organized into three three-year cycles. Children enter at age six, and all students attend the same comprehensive school through grade 9. Assessment is descriptive throughout the first cycle (grades 1–3) and numerical on a 1–5 scale from grade 4 onward. Students sit the National Assessment of Knowledge (NPZ) in grades 6 and 9.

## A Steep Drop

In PISA 2025, Slovenia scored 460 in mathematics — down 25 points from 2022, slipping below the OECD average for the first time. Reading fell to 445 points, down 24 points since 2022 and a striking 50 points since 2018. Only 64% of 15-year-olds reached baseline reading proficiency. Among countries participating across every PISA cycle, Slovenia recorded one of the sharpest declines. Fourth-grade reading had already dropped by 22 points in PIRLS 2021.

Note: some Slovenian media outlets and the education union SVIZ cited slightly different OECD averages in local coverage; the official PISA 2025 OECD averages are 463 in mathematics and 461 in reading.

## A Polarizing Reform Proposal

On the day the PISA 2025 results were released, Education Minister Borut Rončević outlined a ten-point reform package. Its centerpiece is ending the nine-year devetletka and moving to a “modern eight-year school” (osmoletka) starting in 2028/29. Today's first grade would become a free, compulsory final preschool year (“mala šola”), meaning most children would start grade 1 at age seven. The proposal also calls for updated curricula, changes to the NPZ and secondary leaving exams, “restoring teacher authority,” and focused literacy instruction in grade 1.

For now, the package remains a policy proposal rather than enacted law. Legal scholars have raised constitutional questions, and parts of the political opposition strongly oppose the shift.

## What We Could Borrow

- **A national assessment in grade 6** whose results are publicly reported and tracked over time.
- **Descriptive assessment across the entire first three-year cycle.**
- **A clear warning from Slovenia's drop:** early reading instruction has to be explicit and focused in the first two grades, not stretched thin across an entire three-year cycle.
