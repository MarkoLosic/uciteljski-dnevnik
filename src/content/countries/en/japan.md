## How School Is Organized

Japanese children enter elementary school at age six, with the school year beginning in April. Elementary school lasts six years and lower secondary school three. There is no academic tracking before age 15, when upper secondary entrance exams determine students' next steps.

The Ministry of Education (MEXT) sets the national Course of Study and screens textbooks. Prefectural and municipal boards of education run public schools, hire teachers, and rotate them across schools every few years so experienced staff do not cluster only in favored neighborhoods.

## Tokkatsu: The School as a Community

MEXT frames schooling around balanced development of knowledge, character, and physical health. Special activities (tokkatsu) turn that principle into daily habits: children rotate classroom duties, clean classrooms and hallways themselves, hold class council meetings, and serve and eat lunch together. Tokkatsu has become one of Japan's best-known educational exports.

## Lesson Study

Japan's other major export is collaborative lesson study (jugyō kenkyū). Teachers plan a research lesson together, one teacher teaches it while colleagues observe how students respond, and the group meets afterward to analyze and refine the lesson. Professional development happens inside the school, anchored in real classroom practice rather than off-site workshops.

## Large Classes, Long Hours

Japan is gradually lowering the statutory elementary class-size cap from 40 to 35 students, yet classes remain among the largest in the OECD, where the average is around 21. Teachers work notoriously long hours, applicant pools are shrinking, and schools are supporting growing numbers of students with special educational needs and children from non-Japanese-speaking households.

## Student Results

In PISA 2025, Japan ranked first among all OECD countries across every domain: 525 in mathematics, 503 in reading, and 538 in science. Compared with 2022, only the dip in reading was statistically significant. In TIMSS 2023, fourth-graders scored 591 in mathematics. The OECD counts Japan among the seven systems globally that combine top achievement with strong equity.

## What Is Changing

On October 5, 2026, the Central Council for Education's curriculum subcommittee published draft guidelines for the next Course of Study, emphasizing stronger computing education, assessment reform, and a digital curriculum architecture. Published timelines vary between phased rollout from 2028 and full elementary implementation around 2030. Earlier, under the pandemic-era GIGA School Program, Japan equipped nearly every student with a personal learning device within roughly two years.

## What We Could Borrow

- **School-based lesson study**, where teachers plan, observe, and improve actual lessons together instead of sitting through disconnected seminars.
- **Giving children real responsibility for their classroom and school** through rotating duties, tidying up, and class councils.
- **A low-stakes grade 6 national assessment** used to monitor the system rather than punish students or schools.
