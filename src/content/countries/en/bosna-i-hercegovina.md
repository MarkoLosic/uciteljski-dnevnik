## Why Bosnia and Herzegovina Is on This List

Bosnia and Herzegovina is the reference point here. Next to every other country, the practical question is what they do differently than we do — and whether those choices would make sense at home.

## A System Split into Thirteen Parts

Education in BiH is not run by a single ministry. Authority sits with Republika Srpska, the ten cantons of the Federation of BiH, and Brčko District, meaning schools are funded through 13 separate budgets. The state-level agency APOSO has no executive authority over curricula. It develops and monitors the Common Core Curriculum Defined on Learning Outcomes, yet according to the European Commission (2025), that core “remains incomplete and is not applied countrywide.” In practice, schools work under three separate curricula: Bosnian, Croatian, and Serbian.

## “Two Schools Under One Roof”

The most recent complete count comes from a 2018 OSCE report documenting 56 such schools across 28 locations, all within the Federation of BiH and concentrated mainly in Central Bosnia and Herzegovina-Neretva cantons. In September 2026, the OSCE warned again that “the temporary has become permanent.” Cantonal authorities still have not enforced the Federation Supreme Court rulings from 2014 and 2021.

## Fewer Children Every Year

In the 2025/26 school year, primary schools in BiH enrolled 248,585 students across 1,622 schools, including 26,962 first-graders. That is 3,171 fewer students than a year earlier, while the number of schools fell by 45 over three years. As a result, BiH has large numbers of small and multi-grade classes. The core problem is not a shortage of classroom teachers, but a school network that has not adjusted to demographic decline.

## What We Know About Student Learning

In TIMSS 2023, fourth-graders scored 447 in mathematics and 448 in science — well below the international centerpoint. Only 1% of students reached the advanced benchmark in mathematics. Because Republika Srpska and West Herzegovina Canton did not take part, even this result does not represent the entire country.

Bosnia and Herzegovina participated in PISA only once, in 2018. At that time, 58% of 15-year-olds fell below baseline proficiency in mathematics and 54% in reading. The average student performed roughly at the level of a 12-year-old in OECD countries. BiH missed PISA 2022, PISA 2025, and PIRLS 2021. As the European Commission notes, there is “no mechanism for systematic measurement or monitoring of education quality” in BiH.

## What Is Changing

Sarajevo Canton was the first to introduce an outcomes-based curriculum, now rolled out across all nine grades alongside training for more than 4,500 school staff. Herzegovina-Neretva Canton is expanding new curricula after a five-school pilot, and Republika Srpska is implementing its 2022–2030 education development strategy. BiH is registered to take part in TIMSS 2027.

## What Would Help

- **Countrywide participation in PISA, TIMSS, and PIRLS**, insulated from day-to-day political bargaining.
- **Low-stakes external assessment in grade 3 or 4** and a mandatory exit exam at the end of grade 9.
- **Shared learning outcomes in mathematics, science, and literacy** across all 13 jurisdictions, alongside the three language curricula.
- **Teacher licensing, structured mentoring, and mandatory professional development.**
- **Ending “two schools under one roof”** and redirecting savings from tiny classes into teaching assistants, speech therapists, and early literacy support.
