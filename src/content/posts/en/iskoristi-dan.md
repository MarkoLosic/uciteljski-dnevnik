## Introduction

A teacher's work doesn't stop when the bell rings. Planning, registers, messages to parents and thoughts about the children who got stuck easily spill into evenings and weekends. In my article on [primary teaching](/en/blog/ucitelj-i-razredna-nastava) I wrote that an exhausted teacher can't create an inspiring classroom. The reverse is true too: a teacher who has rested comes back to the classroom with more patience.

That's why I'm sharing a poster with ten simple reminders. None of them is new, but we forget them precisely when we need them most. Print it and put it up in the staffroom, next to your desk or on the fridge.

## Ten reminders

1. **Relax.** Forget about problems, frustration, dissatisfaction, stress and pressure. Listen to music, sing, dance and do what you love. Be creative.
2. **Rest** from hard work, long days, difficult situations and people, cooking, cleaning, shouting and rushing. Five minutes is better than nothing.
3. **Give** your time, a compliment, advice, support, a smile, love, hope, friendship, knowledge, cake.
4. **Let go** of guilt, wrong expectations, disappointment, pain, other people's unreasonable demands, judgement, false assumptions, feeling guilty and unkind words.
5. **Find** your place where you feel at peace: in a garden, in the corner of a café, in a park, at a viewpoint. Find your person, your writer, your musician.
6. **Hug** a dog, a cat, someone you love, a friend, someone in need.
7. **Take a walk** and breathe deeply. Stay connected to nature: look at the sky, the sun, the flowers, the butterflies, go for a bike ride. Live healthily.
8. **Think positively** and run as fast as you can from energy vampires and pessimists. Surround yourself with positive people, things and situations. Respect yourself.
9. **Show** your feelings. Spend as much time as you can with the people you love, respect their needs and love, and show them how you feel at every moment.
10. **Laugh** hard, long and loud, with a reason and without one, from the heart. Listen to and tell jokes, watch comedies, do something crazy.

**Live your life today — don't wait!**

## In the classroom too

The poster isn't only for adults. With older pupils, the reminders can start a good conversation in a PSHE or class meeting: which one is easiest for you, and which is hardest? What does "five minutes is better than nothing" mean? Who could you give something to today?

For younger children, one reminder a week is enough — "Hug" or "Laugh", for example — together with a drawing the children make themselves.

## Conclusion

None of these reminders takes much time or money. They only ask us to stop for a moment. And on days full of obligations, that is often the hardest part.
