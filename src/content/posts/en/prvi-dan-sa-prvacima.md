The day I found out I had been hired as a teacher at Grahovo Primary School was one of the happiest days of my life.

And then came the evening before the school year started, and with it, fear.

## Eight little heads

In the morning I walked into the classroom and saw eight little heads and eight pairs of the most beautiful eyes in the world. In that moment I knew this was the most sacred calling a person can have. That's how it all began.

## Agreement first

We didn't start the first day with letters or numbers. We spent it getting to know each other.

Together we agreed on how we would work with one another, and that in our class everything would be done by agreement.

## Getting-to-know-you games

The first day with first-graders isn't a day for textbooks. A six-year-old doesn't yet know who is sitting next to them or what is expected of them, and getting to know each other works best through play. Short games in a circle work best, where every child gets a turn to speak and nobody has to be "the best". Here are a few that work well with this age group.

### The ball of yarn

The children sit in a circle. Whoever holds the ball of yarn says their name and what they like most, keeps hold of the thread and passes the ball to someone else. At the end, there is a web stretched between everyone. It's a lovely moment to say: from today, this is how we are connected, all of us together.

### Name and move

Everyone says their name and makes a movement with it: a wave, a jump, a spin. The whole class repeats the name and the movement. Children remember each other's names much faster this way, and shy children can show a movement even when talking feels hard.

### The sun shines on everyone who…

The teacher says, "The sun shines on everyone who has a dog," and everyone who has a dog stands up and swaps places. Then "on everyone who likes ice cream", "on everyone who has a sister"… The children quickly see what they have in common, and the laughter comes by itself.

### Draw yourself

Every child draws themselves and the thing they love most: a pet, a toy, a favourite food. The drawings go up on the wall, and on the very first day the class has its own "wall of us". For children who can't write yet, a drawing is the easiest way to say something about themselves.

### The agreement as a game

Agreeing on how we'll work together doesn't have to be a list of don'ts. The children can suggest the rules themselves: "we listen when someone else is talking", "we help each other". Each rule gets a drawing, and every child "signs" it with a drawing or a handprint. Children find it much easier to keep rules they came up with themselves.

## That evening

That evening I told my friends about my first experiences in the classroom. And I told them this was the most precious thing that had happened to me so far.
